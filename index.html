When blood takes a shortcut

By Freddie

A study of drone blood delivery in Rwanda found a 51% reduction in the in-hospital mortality rate from severe bleeding after childbirth at hospitals using the service.

What changes when urgently needed blood can travel over the hills? This story map explores the geography behind faster delivery, comparing road journeys and drone flight times.

Source: Wharton research summary, May 2026.

What does the finding mean?

The study reports a relative reduction in in-hospital mortality from postpartum hemorrhage, or severe bleeding after childbirth. It does not measure a reduction in all maternal deaths in Rwanda. The route comparisons below illustrate travel-time differences; they do not measure lives saved at the mapped facilities.

Scroll to follow the journey.

A short distance. A longer journey.

Rwanda’s hills can turn a short distance into a long road trip. Before drone delivery, hospital staff sometimes spent hours traveling to regional blood banks to collect supplies.

Blood available at a supply center still needs to reach the care team. The route matters. Start with the wider medical-delivery network, then follow one journey from Muhanga to Ruli.

About this comparison

These records are screened distribution-center-to-site pairs, not a national facility count. Names identify likely healthcare sites; blood-service eligibility is not verified. Failed road routes, large endpoint gaps and duplicate-coordinate records are excluded.

Road estimates use OSRM and OpenStreetMap, September 20, 2026. Both road and flight estimates describe outbound travel only, excluding preparation, waiting, return travel and unmapped access. Thirty minutes is an illustrative threshold, not a clinical deadline.

How this journey was estimated

This is a modeled road delivery from Muhanga, not a recorded trip or a reconstruction of historical collection from a blood bank. Preparation, waiting, return travel and unmodeled access gaps are excluded.

The six selected examples use OpenRouteService roads based on OpenStreetMap and selected planned outbound flights. The network overviews use OSRM roads and average outbound flight distances from the supplied route statistics, so estimates can differ.

Same destination. Different journey.

The planned flight to Ruli takes an estimated 26.7 minutes, compared with 111.7 minutes by road. That is a modeled travel-time difference of 85 minutes.

Both journeys start at Muhanga. The solid road stays visible as the dashed flight path takes a more direct course toward Ruli.

Faster delivery. More timely care.

The study behind the opening finding also found greater mortality reductions at hospitals closer to drone ports. Delivery locations still matter, even when supplies can travel by air.
